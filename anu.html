Digital Companion

An interactive digital experience designed to help families explore the ANU Museum of the Jewish People through games, challenges, and personal identity-building activities.

Context

Rethinking the Museum Visit

Families visiting the ANU Museum on their own may move passively through the exhibits, feel overwhelmed by information, or struggle to connect historical objects to their own lives, especially without a guided tour to provide context. I saw an opportunity to rethink the independent family visit by creating a self-guided interactive “quest” experience that makes the museum more engaging, personal, and meaningful while encouraging families to explore, discuss, and connect with the objects they encounter.

Research

Competitor Analysis

I researched museum experiences, interactive games, and potential visitor behaviors to understand what could make a self-guided visit more engaging. I looked at GooseChase as a reference for location-based scavenger hunts and interactive missions. While its format offered a strong foundation for exploration, it wasn't built specifically around ANU's collection, family learning, or personal identity.

Defining the Experience

I mapped out how the experience could work from beginning to end.

Before designing individual screens, I started thinking about the experience as a journey through the museum.

I mapped how a family would enter the experience, receive a challenge, explore the museum, interact with an object, collect a response, and eventually create something personal from what they discovered.

This helped me turn a broad idea—“make the museum more interactive”—into a clear digital experience with a beginning, middle, and end.

Experience flow — mapping how visitors move through the digital companion.
Design Process

From passive observation to active exploration.

I explored different ways a digital companion could guide families through the museum without taking attention away from the physical collection.

DIRECTION 01

Museum Quest

Visitors receive a QR code when they enter the museum and begin missions throughout the galleries. Instead of memorizing information, the challenges encourage observation, exploration, and discussion.

DIRECTION 02

My Jewish Map / Identity Suitcase

Visitors collect identity fragments as they move through the museum: values, memories, stories, objects, and emotions. These pieces become a visual representation of what they connected with throughout the experience.

Museum Context

The digital experience works alongside the physical museum.

The goal was not to pull visitors away from the museum and put them on a screen. The website acts as a layer over the existing experience, directing families back toward objects, spaces, and conversations within the galleries.

The physical museum becomes part of the interaction.
Digital challenges direct visitors toward physical objects.
Exploring how the experience could be implemented within the museum environment.
The Solution

A digital museum companion that turns the visit into a shared journey.

I designed a website that uses games, challenges, and identity-building activities to help families actively explore the museum and create personal connections to its collection.

Visitors enter through a QR code, complete missions throughout the galleries, collect personal responses, and finish with a digital representation of what they discovered.

  • QR-based entry that works alongside the physical museum experience.
  • Interactive missions encourage visitors to explore objects.
  • Challenges create opportunities for family discussion.
  • Identity fragments connect museum objects to personal experiences.
  • A final digital identity map gives visitors something personal to take away.
Design Outcome

ANU Museum Digital Companion

The final concept transforms a self-guided museum visit into an interactive journey. Instead of simply reading about Jewish history, families explore, make choices, discuss objects, and build their own connections to the collection.

01 — Explore

Start with a mission.

A QR code introduces visitors to the experience and gives families an immediate reason to begin exploring the galleries.

Missions turn museum objects into discoveries rather than assignments.

02 — Discover

Explore objects through challenges.

Instead of memorizing dates and facts, families are asked to look closely and respond to what they see.

A challenge might ask: Find an object older than 1,000 years. Find something that surprised you. Which object would you bring home—and why?

03 — Connect

Make the collection personal.

Visitors collect identity fragments as they move through the museum—values, memories, stories, objects, and emotions.

This encourages families to talk about why particular objects resonate with them rather than simply moving from one label to the next.

04 — Reflect

Leave with your own story.

At the end of the experience, the fragments collected throughout the museum become a personalized digital identity map.

The final experience gives visitors a visual record of the objects, ideas, feelings, and connections they discovered along the way.

Implementation

Designing for the real museum, not just the screen.

I considered how the digital experience could physically live inside ANU. QR codes could be placed at entry points and near relevant objects, allowing the digital challenges to direct visitors back into the galleries.

The experience is intentionally lightweight: visitors use their own phones, while the museum environment remains the primary place of discovery.

Impact

Shifting the museum experience from learning about Jewish history to discovering where I fit within it.

The experience creates a stronger connection between ANU's collections, educational programming, and visitors by making exploration active, social, and personal.

Transform visitors from passive observers into active participants.
Increase engagement and time spent exploring exhibits.
Encourage conversations between family members and generations.
Help visitors understand Jewish identity as diverse, personal, and evolving.
Connect museum collections with educational programming.
Provide meaningful guidance without requiring a staff member for every visitor.

The goal isn't just to help families learn about Jewish history. It's to help them discover where they fit within it.
